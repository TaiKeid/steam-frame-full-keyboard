# Changelog

## Unreleased

- Shift taps, summoning the keyboard and the laser crossing the toolbar's buttons redraw the keyboard about five times faster on the Frame, in about 6 ms instead of about 30: every key of the same shape now shares one drawing of its body, kept in about 0.6 MB, and the keys' edge light takes one stroke instead of two. Keys now sit on whole pixels, each moved by less than half a pixel.

## 0.18.0: Streaming support and a new look

- Added **Streaming support** to Settings → Integration, off after a manual install and your choice in the installer: Full Keyboard now types into games and applications that Steam streams from your PC, where only Steam's own keyboard worked before and typed nothing but basic Latin text. Press SteamOS's keyboard button with the stream in front, or click the game while the keyboard is showing. Shortcuts and every key go through, with Ctrl or Alt held for a game's own bindings; from a Windows PC, text in any language arrives too. A Linux PC takes keys and shortcuts but ignores text Steam sends it, so only what keys type arrives there. VR apps streamed from a PC stay unsupported. It needs Replace native keyboard.
- Streaming support installs an additional library that Steam's streaming client loads, and one setting for Steam's start in your own config folder. No file of Steam or SteamOS is changed. Ticking it explains this and asks first, and it takes effect after SteamOS restarts once. Turning it off needs no restart, and neither does turning it back on until SteamOS restarts. A check before every Steam start uses the library only if it loads, and otherwise leaves Steam as it comes. There is a very small risk that streaming stops working after a SteamOS update: turn the setting off, which applies to the next stream, and wait for a Full Keyboard update. See [streamed games](docs/streamed-games.md#through-steams-own-streaming-streaming-support).
- The installer asks about Streaming support on a new last page, for versions from 0.18.0 on, and its Finish page offers **Restart SteamOS**, unticked, when a restart is needed for it. Leaving Settings with ← after ticking Streaming support asks whether to turn it on, after the question about changed languages.
- With Full Keyboard installed, the installer's main button now says what the chosen version does: **Update** for a newer one, **Downgrade** for an older one and **Reinstall** for the same one, instead of always Reinstall. As with an update in Settings, it asks nothing and keeps your settings, so new features such as Streaming support stay off until you turn them on. Its last page asks you to wait while it installs, and says when it's done. Downgrading to a version without Streaming support takes it out of Steam's start, since that version couldn't remove it; updating again sets it up again.
- The keys have a more finished look in every theme but High Contrast. Each key and button catches a faint light along its top edge; modifier, editing, navigation and function keys sit a tone darker than the keys that type characters, so the letter block stands out; the Shift character over a key is dimmer than what the key types; and latched keys and switched-on buttons are shaded like the other keys instead of filled flat. Neon gets the same, and keeps its tubes on pressed and latched keys. Every colour derives from the theme, so themes of your own get the look too. The installer's pictures show the new look as well.
- A key held down now sinks into shade, so a press reads apart from hover at a distance; on Neon it lights its tube, as before. It also lands a little faster and returns more softly.
- The swipe trail looks calmer and livelier at once. The path you traced is now a thin, faint line, smoothed against the laser's tremble, so the letters under it stay readable; a brighter comet rides over its last moments, thinning and fading toward its tail; and when the swipe ends the path fades out instead of vanishing. On Neon the path is purple and the comet a cyan tube. Before, the trail was one thick grey line that struck through every legend it crossed.
- The toolbar's icon buttons, and × at the right, now say what they do: a tooltip shows above the keyboard while the laser is on one and goes as soon as it leaves. The hidden window tooltip of Focus mode takes its place while it shows.
- Added **Hover fade** to Settings → General, under Background, on by default: a key the laser leaves dims over a moment, a short trail behind the laser, instead of going dark at once. High Contrast keeps its keys lighting and going dark at once. Turn it off to have keys go dark at once, as before. With the new row, Auto leveling is below the General card's fold until you scroll the card.
- A key you let go of while the keyboard was hidden no longer plays its release when the keyboard shows again.

## 0.17.0: Chinese, installer and more languages

- An installer now comes with every release: `Full_Keyboard_installer-aarch64.AppImage`, linked from the **Quick install** button at the top of the README. Download it on the Frame, open its folder in Dolphin and double-click it, also in Game Mode; no Konsole and no typing. It installs the newest release or one chosen from 0.16.0 on, sets up a first install with Replace native keyboard, swipe typing, typing suggestions, Pull for Shift and the numpad while it downloads, then opens the keyboard when it replaces Steam's. With Full Keyboard installed, it reinstalls or uninstalls it, keeping settings if you choose. It first came, unannounced, with 0.16.0; see [the installer](docs/installer.md).
- Chinese now converts with libime for Pinyin, with OpenCC's Taiwan phrases for Traditional Pinyin, and with McBopomofo for Zhuyin, instead of PyZy. Zhuyin takes tones as written in the dictionary or as spoken (不客氣 with ㄅㄨˋ or ㄅㄨˊ). A Traditional Pinyin candidate you select keeps exactly the characters shown, and the rest converts with it as context: 皇, then the rest, stays 皇后. The dictionaries load only while a Chinese language is added; Traditional Pinyin needs about 50 MB more while it is. libime's language model is pruned to keep the download under the 64 MiB that 0.16.0 updates to, which costs about two points of whole-sentence accuracy. See [Chinese engines](docs/languages.md#chinese-engines).
- Chinese learns, as Steam's keyboard does: Pinyin the words you choose and the sentences you commit, Zhuyin the candidates you pick over its conversion, kept across restarts in the `chinese/` folder of the config folder. Nothing is learned in a password, PIN or terminal field or in a preview. **Clear learned words** in Settings → Languages and Layouts lists Chinese and forgets it, keeping a reading you left open.
- Chinese types as PC input methods do. Punctuation keys type Chinese punctuation, ，。？！；：、 and quotes in turn, with 【】《》￥…… in Simplified and 「」『』 in Traditional; after a digit, . , and : type themselves, for 3.5, 1,000 and 10:30. In Pinyin, Enter types the letters as typed (`steam`) instead of the conversion, Shift and Caps Lock letters type themselves at once, also with swipe typing on, and - and = page the candidates. A reading past 30 keys or syllables sends its start and goes on instead of refusing keys, and a third Quick key types the highlighted character and starts the next code.
- Zhuyin works as McBopomofo does on a PC: the reading has a cursor, moved by ←/→, Home and End, and the candidates are for the phrase before it. Space or ↓ starts choosing, 1–5 or Enter pick, and a pick keeps the reading open to fix other phrases; only Enter sends the line. Punctuation goes in at the cursor, Delete takes the syllable after it, and Esc steps back one stage at a time. Before, Space chose the highlighted candidate.
- Cangjie and Quick offer common characters first. On tables without frequencies, such as the desktop simulator's, Quick hi now offers 我 first instead of 68th; on SteamOS's own tables the order only breaks ties. Rare forms, such as characters that often show as boxes, come after a code's common completions, and each character is listed once.
- Japanese and Chinese no longer learn in a terminal the keyboard recognises on the desktop, such as Konsole, and Japanese doesn't offer there what it learned elsewhere; before, only a shell's terminal hint stopped them. Typing suggestions already worked this way.
- In-app updates no longer limit the size of a release or the time its download takes; a download that stops receiving data for two minutes still ends with an error. Releases from 0.16.0 and earlier keep their 64 MiB limit, so releases stay under it while people update from them.
- Chinese ranking experiments for development are built in, off by default and without model weights; see the [simulator guide](docs/simulator.md#chinese-candidates).
- The interface is now also in Czech, Latin American Spanish and European Portuguese. Steam's Spanish - Latin America setting, or a locale such as `es_MX` or `es_AR`, now picks Latin American Spanish instead of Spain's, and Steam's Portuguese setting or `pt_PT` picks European Portuguese instead of Brazilian. The translations are AI-made; corrections are welcome.
- Swipe typing and typing suggestions now work in 25 more languages: Czech, Slovak, Danish, Norwegian, Finnish, Estonian, Latvian, Lithuanian, Hungarian, Romanian, Croatian, Serbian (Cyrillic and Latin), Slovenian, Bulgarian, Macedonian, Greek, Turkish, Catalan, Icelandic, Indonesian, Filipino, Vietnamese, Azerbaijani, Arabic and Hebrew. Their word lists hold 50,000 words each, about 0.6 MB, and only the language in use is loaded. Vietnamese đ is swiped on D, and a tone mark keeps ư and ơ on their own keys; Turkish and Azerbaijani I is swiped on ı, and Shift, Caps Lock and suggestions follow their casing: i capitalises to İ, and İ completes to İyi. Arabic ذ, on the number row, is swiped on د. On Bulgarian's two phonetic layouts, words with ю or ч (typed on the number row) can't be swiped.
- Added a **Key vibration** checkbox to Settings → Typing. Unchecked, the keyboard's own vibration stops: the hover tick, the stronger press, the release click and the Pull for Shift tick. The controller's own click on trigger press stays, and key sounds and dictation's listening cues keep their own switches. Saved as the optional global boolean `vibration`, on by default, applied with Apply and save. Thanks to @netux for the change (#13).

## 0.16.0: Interface languages and fixes

- The keyboard's own interface (Settings, messages, tooltips and dialogs) now follows the system language: Simplified and Traditional Chinese, Russian, Spanish, Brazilian Portuguese, German, Japanese, French, Polish, Korean, Turkish, Ukrainian and Italian, with English otherwise. On SteamOS the system language is Steam's own language setting. Keyboard layouts and key legends follow the typing language as before. The translations are AI-made; corrections are welcome. See [localization design](docs/localization-design.md).
- Renamed **Flick down for Shift characters** in Settings → Languages and Layouts to **Pull for Shift**, with the second line: pull a key down to type its Shift character.
- The README is now also in Simplified Chinese, Russian, Spanish, Brazilian Portuguese, German and French, besides English and Japanese.
- Typing into PC apps and games streamed through Moonlight now works beyond plain letters. Letters, digits, symbols, Enter, Backspace, the arrows and shortcuts reached the PC as keys already; other text, such as é, Cyrillic, emoji, and Japanese, Chinese and Korean, arrived as digits. It now goes a character at a time through the Frame's clipboard and Moonlight's own Ctrl+Alt+Shift+V, which types it on the PC. Picking a swipe alternative now replaces the word there instead of deleting one letter, and dictation types its text instead of pasting the PC's own clipboard. See [streamed games](docs/streamed-games.md).
- Added typing suggestions, as on a phone: while you type a word, up to five completions show in the toolbar, where swipe alternatives go, and choosing one types the rest of the word and a space. Hel offers Help, Hello, Held, Hell and Helped; HEL offers them in capitals. They come from the swipe word lists, so they work in the same languages as swipe typing, without turning swiping on. Words you finish or swipe rank higher the more you use them, and a word the list lacks is offered once you have used it three times. Swear words and slurs come after every other completion until you have used them yourself, then rank like any word; swipe typing still offers them as before, since a swipe spells the whole word; nothing is learned from words ended by Enter, Tab or a digit, or in fields marked as password, PIN or terminal. On by default; turn it off in Settings → Languages and Layouts → **Typing suggestions**. See [typing suggestions](docs/configuration.md#typing-suggestions).
- **Clear learned words** in Settings → Languages and Layouts is now always there, and asks which languages to forget: each language with something learned has a switch, all off, with how many words you typed, and Japanese with the conversions you chose. Before, it showed only with Japanese and cleared all of Japanese at once.
- Japanese: phrases you converted before are offered again while you type their start, as before 0.15.0: typing あり offers 有難う once you have written it. Since Mozc took over converting, only phrases Anthy had learned were offered.
- Added a **High Contrast** theme for low vision, in Settings → General → Theme: white legends and bright white edges on black keys, a yellow edge where the laser points, solid yellow while a key is held down and cyan when it is latched or switched on. All text is at least 7:1 against its background, disabled controls included, and Settings, the candidates, the emoji picker and the dictation editor follow it. Themes can draw such edges with the new optional `edge`, `edge_hover`, `pressed` and `pressed_legend` fields.
- Cut, Copy and Paste now work in terminals, opened from the dashboard or in SteamOS's Desktop: in Konsole and most other terminals they press Ctrl+Shift+C and Ctrl+Shift+V instead of Ctrl+C, which stopped the running command, and Ctrl+V, which pasted nothing. Cut shows disabled there, as a terminal has no cut, and in xterm, which copies by selecting, all three do. Dictation's paste uses the same keys. See [terminals](docs/runtime.md).
- On the flick layout, 確定 now goes through a conversion one segment at a time, as on Japanese phones: it settles the highlighted segment and moves on to the next, and in the last one commits the phrase. がっこうはむずかしい shows 「学校」は難しい, then 学校「は」難しい, then 学校は「難しい」, and the next 確定 types it. ⌫ steps back a segment, keeping the choices made, and returns to the reading only from the first. On full layouts Enter still commits the whole phrase at once, as PC input methods do. Thanks to @Malorn44 for the report (#10).
- Renamed **Use Drag to Move** in Settings → General to **Grab to move**, with a second line saying what it does: hold grip on the keyboard to move it instead of the line below it. The saved setting is unchanged. Thanks to @Bisonator for the report (#12).
- Fixed: a Full Keyboard started from SteamOS's Desktop, such as from its terminal, typed only into Steam's own fields, not into apps such as Kate. It watched the Desktop's display for which app has focus, instead of Steam's. Started there, it now takes Steam's session display, runtime folder and bus from SteamOS's service manager, and works as if opened from the dashboard.
- Fixed: **Start with SteamOS** couldn't be turned on or off in a Full Keyboard started from SteamOS's Desktop, such as from its terminal: **Apply and save** said "Could not refresh SteamOS startup settings" and the checkbox went back. Apps started in the Desktop get a runtime folder and session bus of their own, where `systemctl --user` can't reach SteamOS's service manager. The keyboard now asks the manager to reload through the main session bus when that happens. Thanks to @jlsalvador for the fix (#11).
- Fixed: run in SteamOS's Desktop terminal, the uninstaller couldn't reach SteamOS's service manager either, so it could neither stop the Start with SteamOS service before removing it nor tell the manager it was gone. It now reaches the manager through the main session bus too.
- Fixed: with 日本語 JIS (system IME), the 半/全 key did nothing, so the desktop's input method could not be switched between Japanese and Latin from the keyboard. It sent the key code that Japanese keymaps leave empty; it now sends the one a JIS keyboard's 半/全 key sends.
- Added **English (US), Colemak-DH**, next to Colemak, whose keys, legends and swipe typing follow that arrangement.

## 0.15.0: Improved typing, swiping and overall feel, and reworked Japanese and Korean

- Shortcuts in Steam's own text fields now say "Shortcuts may not work in Steam fields", instead of pointing to Steam's keyboard, which has no Ctrl or Alt.
- Fixed: with KDE Desktop Unicode set to Paste, text typed into SteamOS's Desktop pasted whatever its clipboard already held, then input stopped with "Text not sent". KWin 6.6 sets the clipboard only through the standard ext data-control protocol, which the keyboard now speaks besides the older one. It also never presses Ctrl+V in the Desktop unless the Desktop's own clipboard holds the text, and dictation's paste types there instead. A clipboard that never finishes being set no longer holds back every later key either: the paste gives up after two seconds, as one nobody takes already did.
- Japanese conversion now uses Mozc, the open-source engine of Google Japanese Input. It weighs the whole sentence, so everyday chat converts right far more often than with Anthy, above all long sentences, and it writes digits, Latin letters and % half-width, as chat does (2日, Tシャツ, 10%). Numbers spelled in kana come as digits too, as chat writes them: さんじゅっぷん is 30分, いちまんえん 1万円, もうよねん もう4年, ゆうがたごじ 夕方5時, かようびのよじ 火曜日の4時, いってんごばい 1.5倍, ぜろきゅうぜろ 090, ろっかいのえれべーたー 6階のエレベーター, while words such as いちばん, いっぱい and 一度 stay. A verb takes the form its object asks, even a few words after it (雪が降って, 写真はちょうど10年前に撮った, ギターを弾いて, 猫を飼って, 目が覚めた, エアコンが効かない, 腰を痛めた), and a noun the form its verb asks (風邪をひく, 咳が出る, 帽子をかぶる, 髪を切った). Words chat writes in kana come in kana where Mozc would write kanji: ください, できる, よかった, もらう, つらい, うちの; and the other way round, ご飯, 疲れた, 暇な時, 待った方がいい. Full Keyboard runs a Mozc of its own only while Japanese is the main or an additional language: it starts when Japanese is added in Settings and stops when Japanese is removed, so it uses no memory otherwise. It runs in a sandbox that can write only its own profile. If it stops answering, Anthy converts until it is back, within seconds. If it keeps stopping soon after it starts, Full Keyboard stops starting it again and Anthy converts until Japanese is next added. It learns your picks too, and **Clear learned words** clears them. See [Mozc](docs/japanese.md#mozc).
- Japanese conversion no longer holds up the keyboard: Mozc and Anthy convert on a thread of their own, so a long sentence no longer freezes the panel and laser for a moment each time candidates come. Typing goes on while they convert. Keys pressed right after 変換, before its candidates are in, wait for them and then apply in the order pressed, so 変換 then Enter still commits the conversion; ⌫, Esc and ↶ pressed straight after 変換 leave it at once instead, as on a phone. A key still held down while they wait is let go in its turn if the laser switches hands, the headset comes off or the panel is grabbed, so it never stays pressed or repeats. Stopping Mozc, as when Japanese is removed, and **Clear learned words** no longer pause the keyboard either.
- Japanese conversion now works the way Japanese phone and PC input methods do. Candidates appear as you type, after every kana, without pressing 変換, and a tap on a list still a kana behind commits that candidate for the part it covers, leaving what you typed after it: the whole phrase, its first segment's candidates, then the reading in hiragana and katakana, with the completions, common phrases, emoji and kaomoji described below. Tapping a candidate chooses it and moves on to the next segment, and in the last segment commits the phrase; Enter still commits everything at once. Segment boundaries can now be changed: on the flick layout ← and → shorten and lengthen the segment being converted, and on other layouts Shift+← and Shift+→ do. Before conversion, ← and → move a cursor through the reading, so a kana can be added or removed anywhere in it. Thanks to @Malorn44 for the detailed report (#10).
- Added Shift characters by pulling: pull a key down before letting go for its Shift character, such as ^ on the 6 key, or with swipe typing off, a letter's capital. While pulled, the Shift character slides into the middle of the key, which fills once release will type it. Kana keys pull to their Shift kana and Korean keys to their Shift jamo, and a dead Shift character composes as usual. Turn it on in Settings → Languages and Layouts → **Flick down for Shift characters**. With it on, character keys type on release and don't repeat; Space, Backspace, Enter and the arrows still act on press. Keys show the Shift character they pull to, such as ㅃ over ㅂ. A toolbar button pauses and resumes it, hiding those characters meanwhile, like the swipe and haptic sounds buttons, and remembers its state across restarts. See [Pull for Shift](docs/configuration.md#pull-for-shift).
- A swipe can now start in the gap between two letters, which used to do nothing. Each letter reaches halfway to the keys beside it while swipe typing is on.
- The number row no longer takes part in swipes, as phone keyboards have none: its keys type on press, and their letters are swiped on the plain letter, as on a phone. Swipe e-c-o-l-e for école on AZERTY, and s for German ß, i for Italian ì, е for Russian ё.
- Two-set Korean now types as a PC's Korean input method does. Each syllable goes into the field as soon as the next one starts, so only the one being typed waits in the reading line, and long runs such as ㅋㅋㅋ… are no longer cut off at 30 jamo with the rest of the keys lost. Backspace takes back the open syllable's jamo, then deletes whole syllables in the field; held, it pauses at the open syllable's end, then deletes on in the field. Enter types the open syllable and sends Enter in one press, instead of needing a second Enter. Esc reaches the app at once and drops only the open syllable, instead of discarding the whole word. Korean no longer shows a typing hint.
- Dropping a language from the list on the main language in Settings → Additional languages now keeps the old main language as the first additional one, instead of removing it.
- An open dropdown list in Settings, such as Language, now scrolls when you press on it and drag, as the cards do, instead of only with the stick or its scrollbar.
- In the dictation editor, a Japanese, Chinese or Korean reading started over a selection now replaces it at once, as on a PC, instead of leaving it highlighted until the reading is committed.
- A half-typed reading in Japanese, Chinese or Korean now waits through Settings: it is still there when you come back, unless you apply another language or layout, which discards it. Before, opening Settings threw it away.
- Added **Extended key collision** to Settings → Typing, on by default: a press between two keys now reaches the nearer one instead of missing.
- Japanese, Chinese and Korean input take less room. On full-size layouts the reading shows centred in a line of its own under the toolbar, and the candidates in the toolbar row, where swipe suggestions go, from the row's left after a clear gap, so the first candidate is always in the same place. On a narrow keyboard the reading line takes the toolbar's second row's place, its buttons at the line's left. The keys are the size they are in other languages, and the flick layout is a row shorter. Before, Chinese and Korean shrank the keys for a tall composing area and left empty space at the sides. The 変換, 確定, ひらがな, カタカナ, Commit, Cancel and arrow buttons are gone; the keys on the keyboard do the same, and every Japanese candidate's list ends with its hiragana and katakana. Candidates scroll sideways by dragging, gliding on after a quick drag as the emoji picker does, instead of paging five at a time, and a still row ends on a whole candidate; Chinese ones are numbered for the 1–5 keys. While there are more than the row shows, ∨ after them opens them all in a menu under the reading line, which stays in view, as tall as its lines up to five, with a scrollbar when they run longer; while it is open the keys wait, and a press outside it closes it without choosing.
- The Japanese flick layout is now four rows, as on a phone, laid out like Gboard's 12-key keyboard, so its keys are much larger: ↶, ←, 😊 and a new 「あA1」 key on the left; ⌫, →, Space and Enter on the right. The function keys, number row, Esc, Tab and Shift are gone from it. 「あA1」 switches between kana, English and digits: a tap moves to the next mode and a flick picks one. English shows a QWERTY page, with Esc, Tab, Shift and arrows, in the same space; digits turn the grid into a number pad with symbols on its flicks. ↶ works like Gboard's: it steps the last kana back through its key's taps, leaves conversion, or straight after a commit brings the phrase back as a reading. While there are more candidates than the row shows, ∨ at its end opens them all over the keys, as on a phone. On the digits page, digits and their symbols join the reading, as on a phone: 3, then じに, converts to 3時に, and ３ and 三 are among the candidates. Switching modes keeps the reading, also to English, whose letters join it as typed: きょうは, then English, then 3じに converts to 今日は3時に. The layout's ⌫ is drawn as on a phone, a tag pointing left with × in it, and the layout is as wide as a full-size keyboard with its numpad hidden, so switching between the two keeps the case's sides. Before, あ / A only changed what the number row typed, and the next kana key switched back.
- The JIS 半/全 and かな keys now type an unfinished Japanese reading before switching, instead of discarding it, and the language button types an unfinished Japanese, Chinese or Korean one.
- Japanese input learns from your typing, as a phone's and Steam's own keyboard do: candidates you choose come first next time, and words you committed before are offered again once you type their start. Full Keyboard customises its suggestions, so it keeps what it learns in a record of its own in your home folder, without disturbing what other keyboards learned; the first time it starts, it begins from a copy of what Steam's keyboard learned. Settings → Languages and Layouts → **Clear learned words** deletes what Full Keyboard learned, after asking, including the next phrases and phrase choices below; Steam's keyboard's record stays as it is. Nothing is learned in fields a shell marks as password, PIN or terminal fields, and Anthy's own conversion log, `ANTHY_HISTORY_FILE`, is switched off, so nothing typed is written outside the learning records. See [Japanese learning](docs/japanese.md#learning).
- A lone ゛ or ゜ is drawn larger, so it can be read, and stays where it sits beside a kana, so ゜ isn't taken for 。 or ○: the flick layout's 小 key hints and flick preview, and the JIS kana layout's ゛ and ゜ keys.
- The emoji picker opens in a Japanese, Chinese or Korean keyboard's own case, at the keys' size, and the keys it shares with the keyboard, Back in the emoji key's place, Cut, Copy and Paste, stay where they are as it opens, as on other layouts.
- The Korean 12-key (천지인) keyboard has been removed; Korean uses two-set Hangul. A saved 천지인 selection, including a favorite or additional language, opens as two-set Korean on US QWERTY.
- **Disabled** is gone from Settings → General → Background: letting the laser through the gaps between keys made presses near a key's edge miss it. A saved Disabled background becomes **Invisible**, which looks the same and takes the laser across the whole case, and so does `disabled` set by a shell over the integration protocol.
- Japanese on full-size layouts types kana only, as Japanese keyboards do: the あ / A button is gone. For English, add an English layout as an additional language; a JIS keyboard's 半/全 and かな keys still switch.
- Chinese types its own script only, as its keyboards do: the 中文 / A button is gone. For English, add an English layout as an additional language and switch with the language button. Two-set Korean's 한 / A button is gone too: the 한/영 key now stands in right Alt's place, as on a Korean PC keyboard, and switches the keys between Hangul and Latin letters, English added or not.
- Japanese live candidates end with emoji for the reading, as on a phone: ねこ offers 🐈 🐱 🐈‍⬛ 😺. They come from the IBus emoji dictionary SteamOS already has.
- Layouts now match one another. Every language keeps the keys at the size and place a Japanese, Chinese or Korean reading line gives them, so switching between English and Romaji on US QWERTY no longer resizes or moves the keys, the toolbar or the case. Every keyboard ends as close under its keys as the Japanese one did, a narrow keyboard's second toolbar row sits at the same height on every layout, Japanese and flick included, and Settings is a little shorter to match. Dictation mode on a Japanese, Chinese or Korean keyboard keeps the keys at their size too, instead of making them larger; its editor takes the reading's space.
- Common Japanese chat phrases convert as people usually write them, until you choose otherwise: いきます to 行きます, かえる to 帰る, ありがとう in hiragana, ちょっとまって to ちょっと待って, short words such as ここ in hiragana rather than 個々 and おれ to 俺, and short replies such as はい, うん and そっか in hiragana. They come first among the live candidates, and 変換 gives them too. Their words keep these forms inside other sentences: すぐいく is すぐ行く and たべにいこう 食べに行こう, while 持っていく stays in kana, as it is written. A word stretched with ー converts as the word (いいよー, ばいばーい), and a loanword typed in kana comes in katakana (コンビニ, デート). Once you choose otherwise, your choice comes first and the usual phrase second; inside a sentence a word follows your latest choice, as on the iPhone.
- Japanese live candidates end with kaomoji for the reading, after its emoji: にこにこ offers ＼(^o^)／ and 64 more, しくしく (T_T) and others, and かお or かおもじ all 442 of Mozc's.
- Japanese live candidates complete a partly typed reading to common words and chat phrases, as phones do: おめでと offers おめでとう and おめでとうございます first, and いただき いただきます. - Choosing a theme in Settings shows it at once; Back returns to the saved theme.
- The keyboard fades in while rising into place when it opens, and fades out when it closes.
- The flick input's preview and the emoji skin tone menu are now raised keycaps and panels with soft shadows, instead of shapes outlined in bright white.
- In Settings, **Manage** on the Favorites row opens a list of your favorite layout and language pairs over the Languages and Layouts card. A bin button removes one pair, and **Clear all** removes them all after asking. Like every Settings change, removals are saved by Apply, and Back keeps your favorites.
- A layout or language name too long for the dropdown list scrolls while the laser is on it.
- The emoji picker has **Favorites**, after Recent. Hold an emoji in Recent, then drag it into Favorites to keep it there. Drag a favorite to move it, or drop it outside Favorites to remove it. A toned emoji's hold still opens its skin tones; keep holding and move to drag it instead.
- Skin tones stay open after you release the hold: click a tone to type it, or click anywhere else to close them.
- The emoji picker waits for a larger movement before it scrolls, so a slightly unsteady hand still picks the emoji it pressed.
- Holding Shift for half a second locks it, like a phone keyboard: every key gets Shift until you press Shift again, which helps when selecting text with the arrows. Shift keys show an arrow before their name, filled while locked.
- Holding Backspace, an arrow or another repeating key now keeps it repeating when the laser drifts off the keyboard, until you release the trigger, so you can watch the text instead of aiming. This needs a Steam Frame controller; other controllers release the key as before.
- Holding Backspace now repeats on an unfinished Japanese, Chinese or Korean reading, including flick input, instead of deleting one character per press. It stops once the reading is empty, so it never goes on to erase text already in the app.
- Moving, resizing, pinning or recentering the keyboard no longer erases an unfinished Japanese, Chinese or Korean reading; it stays ready to convert or commit.
- A tap that trembles just past a key's edge no longer becomes a swipe: a swipe now starts only once the laser has clearly left the key.
- Moving or resizing the keyboard no longer shows a release instruction in the status line.
- In SteamOS's Desktop, Japanese, Chinese, Korean, emoji and other non-ASCII text no longer arrives as digits. The Desktop runs its own compositor, which misread the keys Steam's input method used, so this text is now typed through the Desktop's own input method instead. While it is typed, its Unicode codes show briefly at the cursor. General → **KDE Desktop Unicode** can paste it instead, which is fast but replaces what is in the clipboard. Paste for **Send** and **Type as I speak** also reaches apps in the Desktop now, through its own clipboard.
- Shift+Enter now starts a new line in Steam's friends chat and other multi-line Steam text boxes when Full Keyboard replaces Steam's keyboard; Enter still sends. It used to show "Control unavailable in Steam fields". Other shortcuts in Steam fields remain unavailable.
- Native keyboard replacement is no longer marked experimental.
- The Experimental settings card is now called **Integration**.
- Alternative Steam Frame shells can host Full Keyboard through an experimental integration protocol: a shell launches it with `--hosted FD` and controls it over that inherited connection, showing and placing it, styling it, setting contexts and settings, controlling dictation, forwarding its own pointer, receiving only committed text, and updating it silently. A keyboard you start yourself has no such connection and behaves as before. See [shell integration](docs/integration.md).
- The **Stay open over apps** button now shows its state only by its icon, like Pin, instead of also lighting up when on.
- Fixed **Stay open over apps** turning on by itself when the dashboard closed while the keyboard was open for a field in the dashboard. It turns on by itself only for a keyboard request from a window pinned in the world while the dashboard is closed.
- Fixed Shift or AltGr after an accent key dropping the accent: ´ then Shift+E now gives É instead of E. Every accent key, such as caron, ogonek or ring, now shows its own legend instead of the US one.
- Fixed a retry after a stalled app typing the start of a word twice. Plain-ASCII words go to local apps one character at a time. If the app stopped acknowledging partway through, pressing Enter to retry typed the whole word again, so *hello* could become *hehello*. The retry now types only the characters that had not been acknowledged.

## 0.10.0: Emoji picker and corner resizing

- Added an emoji picker. - Resize the keyboard from its corners, like a Steam window, instead of with the zoom buttons. Aim either controller's laser just outside a bottom corner: a resizer that follows the keyboard's rounded corner fades in there, and fades out as the laser moves away. - Copy and Paste are now equal-height keys below the emoji key, leaving room for a dictation key later.

## 0.9.2: Swiped words type correctly in X11 apps

## 0.9.1: Full Keyboard returns after in-app updates

Updating into this version already takes over a Steam keyboard that opened during the handoff; the automatic return on every update starts with updates from this version onward.

## 0.9.0: Swipe typing, flick input and typing over pinned windows

## 0.8.0: Typing reliability and Steam dashboard fixes

- Made local-app text delivery asynchronous so a slow compositor does not block the keyboard. Input connections recover in the background without replaying old keys or text. Bundled the fixed libei 1.6.0 client to prevent crashes during interrupted input negotiation, without changing system libraries.
- Fixed Steam search typing reaching a local app behind the dashboard. Text stays bound to the selected Steam field, follows switches between supported Steam and local-app targets, and stops when the field loses focus or disappears.
- Improved native keyboard handoff and cleanup when owners change, the helper fails, or Steam's UI stalls. Abandoned installation attempts are cleaned up, native restoration is checked, and repeated failures use bounded retries.
- Fixed the first keyboard request after background startup and SteamVR session restart, and restored replacement readiness after headset sleep or tracking loss without requiring an app relaunch.
- Fixed Escape dismissal in Steam fields and dashboard sessions. Releasing a queued Backspace repeat now completes its delivery without another deletion, preventing a missing acknowledgement from forcing native fallback.
- Removed the routine pending-text notice that flashed during typing. Reduced bridge polling and inspection work while idle, retaining independent recovery checks and bounded diagnostics that never include typed text.
- Preserved saved profile selections when startup temporarily falls back to another supported profile. Missing or incompatible profiles now produce a useful fallback reason, and custom key IDs no longer collide with toolbar controls.
- Fixed Japanese Romaji input depending on cosmetic key labels instead of resolved key symbols, and stopped idle Space from incorrectly starting composition.
- Made Reload wait for pending settings work while the VR loop stays responsive. Unrelated preference changes can still save when startup configuration fails, and storage errors remain visible.
- Manual reinstalls and downgrades now use fresh release directories. Replacement removes only the old marked production release after successful activation, or startup confirmation for in-app updates, while preserving settings and unmarked development releases.
- Refactored VR lifecycle coordination and expanded requirements-based tests for routing, queued cancellation, dismissal, native fallback, recovery and controller ownership.

## 0.5.0: First release

Full Keyboard for Steam Frame is a separately launched alternative keyboard for the Frame dashboard and local apps. It does not replace the stock keyboard or send input to streamed VR applications.

- Composition commits before ordinary spaces, digits and punctuation, with failed-delivery protection.
- Chinese/Korean engines load on demand; missing libraries leave the rest of the keyboard usable.
- Added French, Spanish, Italian, Brazilian ABNT2, Russian and Ukrainian profiles, Simplified/Traditional Pinyin candidates, and Korean two-set Hangul composition. Language settings and system-layout limitations are documented.
- Sticky left Super and momentary native right Super, with monochrome Frame and SteamOS icons.
- Full-size keyboard with F1–F12, arrow and navigation keys, numpad, Ctrl/Alt/Shift, and dedicated Copy/Paste buttons.
- Layout-independent Unicode character delivery, local Shift/Caps/AltGr and accents, plus native navigation, shortcuts and key repeat.
- Controller laser typing, per-controller press/release haptics, and subtle hover feedback.
- Grip movement and rotation, same-hand thumbstick depth adjustment, size controls, and horizon alignment after release.
- Dashboard position and rotation following, saved placement, and relaunch-to-recenter recovery.
- Dashboard-only visibility and input, with cancellation on hiding, tracking loss, or disconnect.
- Editable JSON layouts, languages, and themes, with in-VR selection, favorites, and profile reload.
- English and German profiles, plus Japanese romaji/kana composition, kanji candidates, and an external JIS IME mode.
- Graphite and Midnight themes, a native desktop preview, and image export.
- Incremental key redraws and native pixel uploads where supported, with an RGBA compatibility fallback and event-driven idle rendering.
- User-local ARM64 installation, preserved settings, versioned rollback, release checksums, and manual installation instructions.
- MIT-licensed project code, build documentation, and automated regression tests.

See the [README](README.md) for installation and controls, and [runtime documentation](docs/runtime.md) for language requirements and known limitations.
