# Changelog

## Unreleased

- Japanese conversion now uses Mozc, the open-source engine of Google Japanese Input. It weighs the whole sentence, so everyday chat converts right far more often than with Anthy, above all long sentences, and it writes digits, Latin letters and % half-width, as chat does (2日, Tシャツ, 10%). Numbers spelled in kana come as digits too, as chat writes them: さんじゅっぷん is 30分, いちまんえん 1万円, もうよねん もう4年, ゆうがたごじ 夕方5時, かようびのよじ 火曜日の4時, いってんごばい 1.5倍, ぜろきゅうぜろ 090, ろっかいのえれべーたー 6階のエレベーター, while words such as いちばん, いっぱい and 一度 stay. A verb takes the form its object asks, even a few words after it (雪が降って, 写真はちょうど10年前に撮った, ギターを弾いて, 猫を飼って, 目が覚めた, エアコンが効かない, 腰を痛めた), and a noun the form its verb asks (風邪をひく, 咳が出る, 帽子をかぶる, 髪を切った). Words chat writes in kana come in kana where Mozc would write kanji: ください, できる, よかった, もらう, つらい, うちの; and the other way round, ご飯, 疲れた, 暇な時, 待った方がいい. Full Keyboard runs a Mozc of its own only while Japanese is the main or an additional language: it starts when Japanese is added in Settings and stops when Japanese is removed, so it uses no memory otherwise. It runs in a sandbox that can write only its own profile. If it stops answering, Anthy converts until it is back, within seconds. It learns your picks too, and **Clear learned words** clears them. See [Mozc](docs/japanese.md#mozc).
- Japanese conversion now works the way Japanese phone and PC input methods do. Candidates appear after a short pause in typing, without pressing 変換: the whole phrase, its first segment's candidates, then the reading in hiragana and katakana, with the completions, common phrases, emoji and kaomoji described below. Tapping a candidate chooses it and moves on to the next segment, and in the last segment commits the phrase; Enter still commits everything at once. Segment boundaries can now be changed: on the flick layout ← and → shorten and lengthen the segment being converted, and on other layouts Shift+← and Shift+→ do. Before conversion, ← and → move a cursor through the reading, so a kana can be added or removed anywhere in it. Thanks to @Malorn44 for the detailed report (#10).
- Added key flicks, as on an iPad: pull a key down before letting go for its Shift character, such as ^ on the 6 key, or with swipe typing off, a letter's capital. While pulled, the Shift character slides into the middle of the key, which fills once release will type it. Kana keys flick to their Shift kana and Korean keys to their Shift jamo, and a dead Shift character composes as usual. Turn it on in Settings → Languages and Layouts → **Flick down for Shift characters**. With it on, character keys type on release and don't repeat; Space, Backspace, Enter and the arrows still act on press. A toolbar button pauses and resumes key flicks, like the swipe and haptic sounds buttons, and remembers its state across restarts. See [key flicks](docs/configuration.md#key-flicks).
- A swipe can now start in the gap between two letters, which used to do nothing. Each letter reaches halfway to the keys beside it while swipe typing is on; a tap there still types nothing.
- Japanese, Chinese and Korean input take less room. On full-size layouts the reading shows centred in a line of its own under the toolbar, and the candidates in the toolbar row, where swipe suggestions go, from the row's left after a clear gap, so the first candidate is always in the same place. On a narrow keyboard the reading line takes the toolbar's second row's place, its buttons at the line's left. The keys are the size they are in other languages, and the flick layout is a row shorter. Before, Chinese and Korean shrank the keys for a tall composing area and left empty space at the sides. The 変換, 確定, ひらがな, カタカナ, Commit, Cancel and arrow buttons are gone; the keys on the keyboard do the same, and every Japanese candidate's list ends with its hiragana and katakana. Candidates scroll sideways by dragging, gliding on after a quick drag as the emoji picker does, instead of paging five at a time, and a still row ends on a whole candidate; Chinese ones are numbered for the 1–5 keys. While there are more than the row shows, ∨ after them opens them all in a menu under the reading line, which stays in view, as tall as its lines up to five, with a scrollbar when they run longer; while it is open the keys wait, and a press outside it closes it without choosing.
- The Japanese flick layout is now four rows, as on a phone, laid out like Gboard's 12-key keyboard, so its keys are much larger: ↶, ←, 😊 and a new 「あA1」 key on the left; ⌫, →, Space and Enter on the right. The function keys, number row, Esc, Tab and Shift are gone from it. 「あA1」 switches between kana, English and digits: a tap moves to the next mode and a flick picks one. English shows a QWERTY page, with Esc, Tab, Shift and arrows, in the same space; digits turn the grid into a number pad with symbols on its flicks. ↶ works like Gboard's: it steps the last kana back through its key's taps, leaves conversion, or straight after a commit brings the phrase back as a reading. While there are more candidates than the row shows, ∨ at its end opens them all over the keys, as on a phone. Before, あ / A only changed what the number row typed, and the next kana key switched back.
- The JIS 半/全 and かな keys now type an unfinished Japanese reading before switching, instead of discarding it, and the language button types an unfinished Japanese, Chinese or Korean one.
- Japanese input learns from your typing, as a phone's and Steam's own keyboard do: candidates you choose come first next time, and words you committed before are offered again once you type their start. Full Keyboard customises its suggestions, so it keeps what it learns in a record of its own in your home folder, without disturbing what other keyboards learned; the first time it starts, it begins from a copy of what Steam's keyboard learned. Settings → Languages and Layouts → **Clear learned words** deletes what Full Keyboard learned, after asking, including the next phrases and phrase choices below; Steam's keyboard's record stays as it is. Nothing is learned in fields a shell marks as password, PIN or terminal fields, and Anthy's own conversion log, `ANTHY_HISTORY_FILE`, is switched off, so nothing typed is written outside the learning records. See [Japanese learning](docs/japanese.md#learning).
- On the Japanese flick layout's digits page, digits and their symbols now join the reading instead of typing at once, as on a phone: 3, then じに, converts to 3時に, and ３ and 三 are among the candidates. Switching modes keeps the reading, as on a phone, also to English, whose letters join an open reading as typed: きょうは, then English, then 3じに converts to 今日は3時に. Before, switching to English typed the reading unconverted.
- The Japanese flick layout is as wide as a full-size keyboard with its numpad hidden, so switching between them keeps the case's sides.
- Key icons on the Japanese flick layout match: Cut, Copy and Paste are the size they are on a full-size layout instead of a third larger, and ↶, 😊, Space and Enter the size of ⌫, beside the ← and → labels. Its ⌫ is drawn as on a phone, a tag pointing left with × in it, on the kana, English and digits pages.
- A lone ゛ or ゜ is drawn larger, so it can be read, and stays where it sits beside a kana, so ゜ isn't taken for 。 or ○: the flick layout's 小 key hints and flick preview, and the JIS kana layout's ゛ and ゜ keys.
- The emoji picker opens in a Japanese keyboard's own case, at the keys' size, instead of widening the case and shrinking back on closing. On full-size Japanese, Chinese and Korean layouts the keys it shares with the keyboard, Back in the emoji key's place, Cut, Copy and Paste, stay where they are as it opens, as on other layouts, instead of jumping up and growing. On the flick layout its Back key takes the side column's top slot, and Cut, Copy and Paste stay in their places and sizes.
- The Korean 12-key (천지인) keyboard has been removed; Korean uses two-set Hangul. A saved 천지인 selection, including a favorite or additional language, opens as two-set Korean on US QWERTY.
- **Disabled** is gone from Settings → General → Background: letting the laser through the gaps between keys made presses near a key's edge miss it. A saved Disabled background becomes **Invisible**, which looks the same and takes the laser across the whole case, and so does `disabled` set by a shell over the integration protocol.
- Japanese on full-size layouts types kana only, as Japanese keyboards do: the あ / A button is gone. For English, add an English layout as an additional language; a JIS keyboard's 半/全 and かな keys still switch.
- Chinese and two-set Korean type their own script only, as their keyboards do: the 中文 / A and 한 / A buttons are gone. For English, add an English layout as an additional language and switch with the language button.
- Japanese live candidates end with emoji for the reading, as on a phone: ねこ offers 🐈 🐱 🐈‍⬛ 😺. They come from the IBus emoji dictionary SteamOS already has.
- With a Japanese, Chinese or Korean language among your languages, the other languages on the same layout keep the keys at the size and place its reading line gives them, so switching between English and Romaji on US QWERTY no longer resizes or moves the keys, the toolbar or the case.
- Common Japanese chat phrases convert as people usually write them, until you choose otherwise: いきます to 行きます, かえる to 帰る, ありがとう in hiragana, ちょっとまって to ちょっと待って, short words such as ここ in hiragana rather than 個々 and おれ to 俺, and short replies such as はい, うん and そっか in hiragana. They come first among the live candidates, and 変換 gives them too. Their words keep these forms inside other sentences: すぐいく is すぐ行く and たべにいこう 食べに行こう, while 持っていく stays in kana, as it is written. A word stretched with ー converts as the word (いいよー, ばいばーい), and a loanword typed in kana comes in katakana (コンビニ, デート). Once you choose otherwise, your choice comes first and the usual phrase second; inside a sentence a word follows your latest choice, as on the iPhone.
- Japanese live candidates end with kaomoji for the reading, after its emoji: にこにこ offers ＼(^o^)／ and 64 more, しくしく (T_T) and others, and かお or かおもじ all 442 of Mozc's.
- Japanese live candidates complete a partly typed reading to common words and chat phrases, as phones do: おめでと offers おめでとう and おめでとうございます first, and いただき いただきます. A reading that is a word itself, such as きみ or あめ, or a word and a particle, such as きょうは, keeps its own conversions first: あめ offers 雨 and 飴 before 編める. The 20,000 words come from Mozc's open-source dictionary.
- After a Japanese commit, the candidate row offers what may come next until the next key, as phones do: phrases you committed straight after this one before, then 。 、 ！ ？. A tap types it at once. **Clear learned words** forgets these phrases too.
- Added **Flick Katakana (JP)** to Settings → Languages and Layouts, off by default: it adds an あ ア key to the Japanese flick layout's side column that switches the kana keys to katakana and back, for typing katakana without converting: a reading typed in katakana is the first candidate. On the digits page the key waits, disabled, still showing the script the kana keys will type. It starts in hiragana and isn't saved.
- Added **Flick Only (JP)** to Settings → Languages and Layouts for the Japanese flick layout, as on phones: with it on, taps never cycle, so a quick second tap of か types かか instead of き. It is off by default, as on iPhone and Gboard.
- On the Japanese JIS layout, the ¥ and ろ keys typed nothing in Latin mode or with romaji. They now type ¥ (Shift |) and \ (Shift _), as a JIS keyboard's do, and are labelled so.
- Japanese romaji reads nn as ん, as Mozc and the iPhone's keyboard do: honnya types ほんや and gennin げんいん. A reading typed so also converts with the n carried over where that reads better, in a sentence too: konnichiha still offers こんにちは, and minna is みんな.
- Japanese romaji types ? ! \ | as ？ ！ ＼ ｜, and the JIS ¥ key's ¥ as ￥, as Japanese input methods do: なんですか？ rather than なんですか?. The half-width forms are among the candidates.
- Japanese conversion keeps ！, ？ and brackets as typed. ちょっとまって！ used to become ちょっと待って! with a half-width !, and a closing 」 became 』. The other forms are still offered after the typed one.
- Dictation mode on a Japanese, Chinese or Korean keyboard keeps the keys at their size. It used to make them larger, since it hides the reading's line; the editor now takes its space instead.
- Keys that can't be used right now look it: they sink flat in a dim colour, as disabled toolbar buttons do, instead of keeping a raised cap with only a dim legend. Dictation mode's Send, Undo, Cut, Copy and Paste while they have nothing to act on, the mic key while a speech model downloads, and the emoji key without emoji.
- The keyboard's messages, such as a key that could not be typed, now show on a card above the keyboard, where the Focus mode tooltip shows, instead of in a line under the toolbar. Beside that tooltip when both show. Settings keeps its messages at the bottom, and dictation mode in its toolbar row.
- The side column's keys are now in the order Cut, Copy and Paste, on every layout and in dictation mode.
- A desktop simulator runs the keyboard in a window on a Linux PC, typing into a text field of its own, for trying it without a Steam Frame. Japanese conversion, ↶, Korean, Chinese, swipe typing and Settings work in it. Its AppImage brings Japanese completion's words and kaomoji, and can bring Mozc and swipe typing, and Anthy, the emoji dictionary and the Cangjie and Quick tables, which the Steam Frame has of its own; a slow field imitates the way Steam's own fields confirm each key. See [the simulator](docs/simulator.md). `--japanese-candidates` prints what Japanese input offers for a list of phrases, kana or romaji, without a window, to check conversion quickly.
- Fixed Steam's keyboard popping up over Full Keyboard while typing fast in the dashboard search. Steam's interface pauses for about half a second while it filters the results. Full Keyboard waited only 0.4 s for each typed piece to be confirmed, and its helper stopped reporting in long enough to be restarted, which handed the field back to Steam. It now waits through such a pause and keeps reporting in while it does. Pauses longer than about 0.8 s still hand the field to Steam's keyboard, as before.
- With **Replace native keyboard** and **Focus mode** on, clicking a field Full Keyboard can't type into, such as the Steam Store's search, now hides Full Keyboard while Steam's keyboard takes over, as **Use Steam keyboard** already did. Before, both keyboards stayed up and typing on Full Keyboard went to the game or app behind the page. Focus mode then holds nothing until the next request opens Full Keyboard. A dictation editor hides the same way and comes back, with its text, when Steam's keyboard closes. The same happens when Full Keyboard loses its connection to Steam while you type in a Steam field, for example while Steam's interface reloads, since Steam's keyboard then comes back for that field.
- With **Replace native keyboard** on, rich-text fields in Steam's own interface, such as a game's notes in its Steam overlay, now open Full Keyboard instead of Steam's keyboard. Before, Steam's keyboard opened, and summoning Full Keyboard over it typed nowhere. Enter starts a new paragraph there and Shift+Enter a new line. Fields inside an embedded web page, such as the Steam Store's search, still use Steam's keyboard.
- With **Replace native keyboard** on, fields in a game's Steam overlay now get Full Keyboard. Steam opens that overlay when the game starts, usually after Full Keyboard is already running, and Full Keyboard only knew the Steam windows that existed when it connected, so those fields got Steam's keyboard. It now picks up Steam windows as they open and lets go of them when they close, including a game's overlay opened again after the game restarts.
- The emoji picker uses less memory and draws faster. It no longer keeps a hidden second copy of every emoji it has shown, which reached about 15 MB after scrolling through all of them, and it draws new emoji about twice as fast. Closing it keeps Recent and Favorites ready for the next opening and frees the rest.
- Turning **Swipe typing** off in Settings now also frees the swipe model, about 3.4 MB, and turning it on loads it again. The toolbar's swipe switch still keeps the model loaded, so switching back is instant.
- With the headset taken off while the keyboard is showing, Full Keyboard idles at four updates a second, and its Steam bridge polls slowly, instead of running at full speed for nobody. Both return to normal as soon as the headset is worn again.
- With **KDE Desktop Unicode** set to paste, a clipboard that never finishes being set no longer holds back every later key. The paste gives up after two seconds, as one nobody takes already did.
- A single invalid optional value in `config.json`, such as a mistyped haptic effect, no longer stops every save ("Could not read saved preferences") or Start with SteamOS. That value falls back as before and is written back corrected, and the values beside it keep theirs: a mistyped `dictation_url` no longer turns dictation off with its server, model, fallback, Type as I speak and recent servers on the next save. Only an unreadable file still blocks saving. **Reload config** now does the same, instead of ignoring every edit while one value was invalid. The dictation server field also no longer accepts more characters than `config.json` can load.
- With **Use Drag to Move** on, the keyboard reads the controllers' grip only while a laser is on the keyboard or a grab is under way, instead of on every frame.
- The native-keyboard recovery helper no longer wakes 40 times a second indefinitely if Steam never reports the keyboard it reopened. After two seconds it keeps watching at its idle pace. Nor does it while you type on the keyboard it reopened: once Steam reports it open, it checks for its close four times a second.
- With a hosted speech service set as `dictation_url` in `config.json`, the Dictation card's **Test** now checks that service, which is where phrases go, rather than the Server address field.
- The uninstaller also removes downloaded speech models when `XDG_DATA_HOME` points somewhere other than `~/.local/share`.
- When native replacement stops working, Settings keeps showing the real reason, such as incompatible Steam interfaces, instead of a generic "connection was lost" after a key that was held in a Steam field is released.
- **Reload config** now updates an open dictation editor's keys after a layout file was edited, and its size after a theme's padding changed. It also takes hand-edited `dictation_url`, `dictation_model` and `dictation_recent_servers` values from `config.json`, and pauses listening, as documented, so phrases spoken after it go to the reloaded server with the reloaded model; phrases already captured finish where they began.
- A dictation helper that stops answering and then never exits is ended after 10 seconds, so dictation and the server **Test** work again without restarting Full Keyboard.
- Shell integration: `dictation.test_server` now answers `busy` while the previous dictation helper is still shutting down, instead of accepting the request and never reporting a result.
- Fixed dictation saying "the headset was asleep" when the headset had only been taken off. It says "the headset was taken off" again, as documented.
- With Replace native keyboard on and the keyboard hidden, Full Keyboard wakes up less often: its stall watchdog ticks four times a second instead of twenty while there is no panel to watch, and the Steam bridge only reports an unchanged idle state four times a second.
- Pointing at the dictation editor uses less CPU. The keyboard checked whether the editor could scroll by laying out its whole text twice per pointer on every frame; it now measures the text only when it changes.
- Dictation's **Fall back to on device** now switches quickly when your speech server is off or asleep. Each phrase used to wait over six seconds for the server first, so a minute of speech could fill the queue and stop dictation; now the server is skipped for 30 seconds after it fails to answer, and live words pause meanwhile.
- Keys that cannot type because Steam's dashboard has the keyboard focus now say so when Replace native keyboard is off: the status line asks you to click the window you want to type into. With replacement on, the Focus mode icon still shakes instead.
- Fixed a possible crash when an integrated shell asked for a Chinese or Korean preview while the keyboard was composing or changing language. Previews and typing no longer use the input engines at the same time.
- Full Keyboard no longer restarts its Steam connection when the connection answered in time but Full Keyboard itself was too busy starting up to read the answer before its three-second limit.
- Fixed Full Keyboard sometimes not returning after an update when its first connection to Steam failed, for example under startup load. The retry now takes over Steam's handed-back keyboard too, instead of leaving it open over Full Keyboard.
- Fixed the keyboard sometimes staying invisible or see-through after it appeared. If the frame that showed it, or the next one, took longer than the 0.18 s fade-in, the fade never finished; the keyboard took input while you could not see it. It now always ends fully visible.
- Fixed native keyboard replacement dropping out for a few seconds when the keyboard was briefly busy, for example after waking. Its Steam bridge helper stopped when the keyboard did not read its replies for about two to twelve seconds; it now waits, keeping the results of keys already sent.
- Fixed a dictated phrase getting stuck when the dictation server **Test** was pressed while that phrase was ending, which held back every later phrase until Esc.
- Fixed dictation's Send continuing into a different field. When Steam's field closed or another field took over between two pieces of a Send, or the keyboard hid and returned, the rest could go to the new target. Send now stops and keeps the rest in the editor, and a Send still waiting for phrases is dropped when the field changes.
- Fixed text being cut off when the keyboard changed view or pointer while it was still typing. Closing the dashboard during Type as I speak, moving or pinning the panel, opening Settings or the emoji picker, or switching language no longer drops the rest of a phrase, a swiped word, a long composition or a dictation Send piece. Held keys are still released at once.
- Fixed text being shortened or dropped when the input connection refused it right after a longer word was typed. Text refused before it is sent is now kept whole, either for an Enter retry or in the composition it came from.
- Fixed app selection sometimes leaving typing blocked because a cached dashboard focus sample discarded the newer click. Focus queries crossing sleep are discarded, and missing controller readings no longer count as app clicks. If focus is lost after waking, click inside the window you want to type into to restore it.
- The keyboard now releases SteamVR mouse input while hidden, asleep or using scene/forwarded pointers. Dashboard mouse input returns only after fresh presentation, rather than retaining runtime interaction while app-side input is blocked.
- Fixed held keys and queued input surviving headset standby or system sleep. Sleep cancels input and sending before wake events are handled, while keeping unsent dictation text in the editor.
- Fixed held editing keys continuing to change dictation text behind the **Discard text?** question. Choosing **Keep** no longer resumes a key held across the question; release and press it again to continue editing.
- Key sounds now warm the audio stream when a pointer enters the keyboard and keep it continuous while either pointer remains there, avoiding repeated stream restarts between clicks. The stream closes two seconds after the last pointer leaves, and immediately when the keyboard hides, the headset is removed or sounds are off or muted. Opening the keyboard alone does not start audio. Settings previews also respect mute.
- Canceling on-device dictation now stops an engine that is still loading its model, so a canceled startup cannot hold up later recognition requests or server tests.
- The dictation server **Test** button now waits for the previous helper to finish cleanup before allowing another test, avoiding a refused retry immediately after helper failure.
- Fixed retained Cangjie and Quick text becoming impossible to send after repeated refused commits. Longer compositions are sent in paced pieces, and a failed submission retains only its unacknowledged remainder for an explicit Enter retry. Retained text is bounded at 1,024 Unicode code points; a full buffer refuses more input without discarding existing text. Backspace with no active reading removes one retained code point instead of clearing the whole composition.
- Fixed swipe encoder cleanup if allocating its adapter fails after the model has loaded. The encoder is now destroyed before its module is unloaded on that failure path.
- The swipe runtime now reuses its process-wide initialization data across encoder lifetimes, avoiding cumulative allocations when its module is repeatedly loaded. Encoder and model instances are still destroyed normally.
- Fixed a small native memory leak when repeatedly initializing Pinyin or Zhuyin. Startup uses private RAM-backed storage that is removed before typing, preserving on-demand dictionary cleanup and disabled learning.

## 0.14.1: The keyboard returns promptly after an update

- Fixed: Full Keyboard returns as soon as an update restarts it, without the five-second wait. An unadoptable handed-back Steam keyboard closes so it cannot remain over Full Keyboard; typing waits for a safe target.

## 0.14.0: Text selection in dictation and a Cut key

- Fixed: Dictation's **Send** no longer presses Enter after the text. It leaves the text in the focused application, so you decide when to submit it. Pressing Enter on the normal keyboard submits it.
- Added a **Cut** key between Copy and Paste on the keyboard. It sends Ctrl+X, and the three keys share the space the two had.
- Added text selection to the dictation editor: Shift with the arrows, Home or End, or Shift with a laser click. Type or dictate to replace the selection. Hold Shift for half a second to lock it while selecting several steps.
- Added **Copy**, **Cut** and **Paste** keys to dictation mode. They work inside the editor only, with their own memory, so the system clipboard is never touched.

## 0.13.1: The keyboard comes back after an update

- After an update you start from Settings, Full Keyboard now shows itself again where it was. With **Replace native keyboard** on, it used to stay hidden until the next keyboard request, so it looked as if it had not restarted. If Steam's own keyboard was open, Full Keyboard takes it over first and then shows, so you don't see two keyboards at once.

Pick a layout on the left, tap or drag languages into your list, drag to reorder, and drop outside to remove. Your main language stays in sync with the Layout and Language dropdowns. Leaving Settings asks whether to save changes made there.
