# Japanese input

Open the Language dropdown in Settings, choose Japanese Romaji, Kana, or JIS, then use Apply and save. For flick input, choose the **Japanese flick 12-key** layout instead; it selects 日本語 — Flick. Kana and JIS need the JIS layout and Flick needs the flick layout: choose the layout first, since Settings dims languages the selected layout cannot type (the open list says so). Choosing a layout that cannot type the current language switches to the closest one, such as Romaji when leaving the flick layout. Existing English/German profiles remain available in the language selector.

| Preset | Entry | Conversion/output |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 日本語 Romaji | Latin letters, such as `nihon` | Local kana composition and [Mozc](#mozc) kanji candidates, Anthy without it |
| 日本語 Kana | Direct JIS-position kana keys | Same local conversion and candidate controls |
| JIS (system IME) | Physical JIS keys, including 変換/無変換/かな | Conversion handled by an already configured target IME |
| 日本語 Flick | 12-key flick grid, as on Japanese phones | Same local conversion and candidate controls |

## Built-in composition

Romaji is the default Japanese choice for someone unfamiliar with Japanese keyboard layouts. It supports common Hepburn and Japanese IME spellings, doubled consonants, `n'` disambiguation, `nn`, and small kana with `x`/`l` prefixes. As in Mozc and the iPhone's keyboard, `nn` is always ん: honnya is ほんや and gennin げんいん, and こんにちは is konnnichiha. A reading typed with ん before a vowel or や, ゆ or よ also offers the words it would make with the n carried over, first while it makes none itself, nor converts whole to one (せんえん is 千円), so konnichiha, read こんいちは, still offers こんにちは first, and tanni offers 単位, then 単に. 変換 and the whole phrase among the live candidates convert such a reading both ways and take the one Anthy splits into fewer segments where the carried n makes words there, as typed on a tie or where it makes none (jikannaru stays 時間ある, not 時間なる): minna is みんな rather than 民|亜, minnadeikou みんなで行こう and nanninkuru 何人来る, while gennin stays 原因, offering the other reading's word second (sannin: 参院, then 三人; konnyaku: 婚約, then こんにゃく), in a sentence too, its whole phrase second among the live candidates (参院来る, then 三人来る). The reading shows as typed until it is converted. xn is ん too. Additional aliases include `ca/ci/cu/ce/co`, `fya/fyu/fyo`, `tha/thi/thu/the/tho`, `dha/dhi/dhu/dhe/dho`, and `wu`, following the corresponding entries in [Mozc's romaji table](https://github.com/google/mozc/blob/master/src/data/preedit/romanji-hiragana.tsv). Punctuation joins the reading in its Japanese form: `-` ー, `,` 、, `.` 。, `[` 「, `]` 」, `/` ・, `?` ？, `!` ！, `\` ＼ and `|` ｜, and on the JIS layout the ¥ key's ¥ as ￥; conversion keeps them as typed and offers the half-width forms among their candidates. Direct Kana has Shift variants for small kana and punctuation; ゛ and ゜ combine with the preceding kana.

On full layouts the reading shows centred in a line of its own under the toolbar, in the place of a narrow keyboard's second toolbar row, whose buttons (such as **Stay open over apps**) sit at the line's left, and the candidates in the toolbar row, after its buttons, where swipe suggestions go: from the row's left, a clear gap after its buttons, so the first candidate is always in the same place, with ∨ after them while there are more. A still row ends on a whole candidate, none cut before ∨; while it is dragged or glides, candidates slide in cut at its edges. ∨ opens all of them in a menu under the reading line, which stays in view, over the keys: as tall as its lines, up to five, with a scrollbar when they run longer; while it is open, the keys take no presses, and a press outside it closes it without choosing. The flick layout instead has a band with a row for the reading, the second row's buttons at its left, and one for the candidates, which ends with ∨ and opens them over the keys. Either stays in every mode, so switching modes never changes the size of the keys, and the case fits the keys with no empty space at the sides. With a Japanese, Chinese or Korean language among your languages on a full layout, the other languages on that layout keep the keys at the size the reading line gives them, with the second toolbar row where the line would be, so switching between them, such as English and Romaji on US QWERTY, moves neither the toolbar, the keys nor the case. Every view of such a layout, the emoji picker and dictation mode too, has the same case. On full layouts its keys are the size they are in other languages, and the case is shorter only by the height they leave spare; the flick layout's case is the second row shorter, and as wide as a full layout's without its numpad. Before anything is typed, a hint shows where the reading goes. The keys do the rest: there are no separate convert, commit, script or cancel buttons.

- **Live candidates.** After a short pause in typing (0.15 s), candidates for the reading appear without pressing 変換: the whole phrase converted, then the candidates of its first segment, then the reading in hiragana and in katakana. While you keep typing, the old list stays dimmed and takes no clicks until the next one is ready. Tapping the whole phrase commits it. Tapping a first segment's candidate chooses it and moves on to the next segment, as in conversion below; tapping hiragana or katakana commits the reading in that script.
- **Emoji.** Live candidates end with up to four emoji for the reading or its likeliest conversions, as on a phone: ねこ offers 🐈 🐱 🐈‍⬛ 😺. They come from the system's IBus emoji dictionary (`/usr/share/ibus/dicts/emoji-ja.dict`, read through libibus, both already on the Steam Frame), loaded on the first live candidates; without them there are none. Tapping one types it in place of the reading.
- **Conversion.** Space or 変換 converts the reading, then moves through the active segment's candidates. On layouts with Shift and arrow keys, Shift+Space or ↑ goes back and ↓ forward. Tapping a candidate chooses it for the active segment and moves on to the next; in the last segment it commits the whole phrase. The active segment is highlighted in the reading. Each segment's list ends with its reading in hiragana and katakana, if Anthy did not already offer them.
- **The cursor.** Before conversion, ← and → move a cursor through the reading, as in a phone's IME: kana, romaji, ⌫, ↶ and ゛゜小 act at it, and the band and the dictation editor show it as a caret. It stays inside the reading. Conversion still takes the whole reading.
- **Segment boundaries.** On the flick layout, as on phones, → makes the active segment one character longer and ← one character shorter; Anthy splits the rest of the reading again, and earlier segments keep their choices. Shift+←/→ moves to the previous or next segment instead. On other layouts, as in PC IMEs, ←/→ move between segments and Shift+←/→ change the active segment's length.
- Enter or 確定 commits the whole phrase as shown. It does **not** submit the target form. Enter with no composition sends a normal physical Enter.
- F6 and F7 change the reading's script to hiragana and katakana. 無変換 restores the hiragana reading.
- Backspace removes a kana/pending roman letter; during conversion it first returns to the reading. Escape first cancels conversion, then clears the reading.
- Full layouts type kana only, as Japanese keyboards do: for English, add an English layout as an additional language and switch with the language button, which types any reading first. On a JIS keyboard, 半/全 and かな still switch between kana composition and Latin keys, typing any reading first. In Latin mode the ¥ key types ¥ (Shift |) and the ろ key \ (Shift _), as a JIS keyboard's do, and shows both; with romaji they join the reading, ¥ \ and | full-width. The flick layout has its own mode key instead; see below.
- Starting another word while converting commits the chosen phrase first. Tab, Delete, Home/End, Copy/Paste, and Ctrl/Alt/Meta shortcuts first commit unfinished composition, then send their physical key events. If committing fails, the text remains and the key or shortcut is not sent. Escape still cancels deliberately.

The preedit stays in the keyboard until committed. Moving the laser off the keyboard keeps it. Opening Settings, dragging, hiding, recentering, changing profiles or losing input focus discards it. This avoids committing an old phrase after a focus transition. Input routing follows compositor focus, just like the ordinary keyboard.

## Flick input

The flick layout is four rows, as on a phone, laid out like Gboard's 12-key keyboard: the kana grid (あ か さ / た な は / ま や ら / ゛゜小 わ 、。?!) with ↶, ←, 😊 and 「あA1」 on its left, and ⌫, →, 空白 (Space) and 改行 (Enter) on its right. Space and Enter show ␣ and ↵; while a reading is open they show 変換 (次候補 while converting) and 確定. It has no function keys, number row, Esc, Tab or Shift; the English page has those, and digits have a mode of their own. Cut, Copy and Paste stay in the side column. Each kana key shows its tap kana with the four flick kana as small hints. The band's candidate row ends with ∨, which opens every candidate over the keys, as a phone's does: the row keeps the ones that fit, and the rest wrap into lines below it that scroll up and down with a drag. Tapping one chooses it and closes the list; ∧, in ∨'s place, or a press anywhere else closes it without choosing, and the keys take no presses meanwhile. The kana page has no ↑ and ↓ for the field's cursor; the English page has them.

- Tap a key for its first kana. Hold the trigger, move left, up, right or down by about 40% of a key's height and release for that direction's kana (あ: い う え お). While held, a cross-shaped preview over the key shows the tap kana in the middle and the four flick kana around it, highlighting the one release will type; moving back to the centre returns to the tap kana. The preview lies above everything else, may extend past the keyboard edge, and the laser keeps tracking over it.
- Toggle input: tapping the same key again within one second replaces the kana with the next in its row, then small kana (あ い う え お ぁ … ). → ends the cycle, so the next tap types the first kana again. With **Flick Only (JP)** on (Settings → Languages and Layouts; off by default, as on iPhone and Gboard), taps never cycle: a quick second tap of か types かか.
- Katakana: with **Flick Katakana (JP)** on (Settings → Languages and Layouts; off by default), an あ ア key above Cut switches the kana keys to katakana, the one in use lit as on the 「あA1」 key. It is a switch: a tap turns it to the other script. The keys, their hints and the flick preview then show katakana, and typing, toggle input, ゛゜小 and ↶ work in katakana. A reading typed all in katakana is the first live candidate, as typed (ガッ). On the digits page it is disabled, drawn flat like a disabled button, still showing the script the kana page will type. It is not saved: the keyboard starts in hiragana, and a change of language or layout returns it there. Dictation mode gives its place to Esc.
- ↶ works as Gboard's 逆トグル／戻す key. While typing, it steps the last kana back through its key's taps (う い あ, then on to ぉ), and a tap after it goes on from there. While converting, it returns to the reading. Straight after a phrase is committed, it takes the phrase back out of the field (with Backspace, one per character) and opens it as a reading again; any other key, the laser leaving the keyboard or the keyboard hiding ends that chance, since the field's cursor might no longer be after the phrase. While the phrase is still being typed, ↶ says so and keeps the chance for a tap once it is in. The reading appears once the field has taken every Backspace; until then keys, A/あ, the language button and Settings wait. A field that goes three seconds without answering is given up on, with a message, and the keys work again; a slow field that keeps answering is waited for. It is not the dictation editor's Undo.
- ゛゜小 cycles the previous kana through its small, voiced and semi-voiced forms (つ っ づ, は ば ぱ); flick left for ゛, up for 小 and right for ゜. や flicks to 「 and 」, わ to を, ん and ー, and 、。 to 。, ？, ！ and ….
- 「あA1」 switches between kana, English and digits; its lit character shows which is on. A tap moves on to the next mode; a flick picks one: left for kana, up for English, right for digits. The three share the reading, as on a phone: きょうは, then English, then 3じに still converts to 今日は3時に.
- **English** shows the layout's English page: a QWERTY keyboard with Esc, Tab, Shift, ⌫, ↵ and the arrows, in the same case as the kana page, so nothing moves or resizes. Its letters type at once, or with a reading open, join it as typed: Space converts it and Enter commits it, as on the kana page. Swipe typing is not available on it.
- **Digits** turns the grid into a number pad, 1–9 with ( 0 . on the bottom row. A tap adds the digit to the reading and a flick its symbols, as on a phone's number mode, so 3 and then じに on the kana page convert to 3時に, with ３時に and 三時に among the candidates; Enter commits digits typed alone. The symbols: 2 flicks to ¥ $ €, 5 to + × ÷, 0 to 〜 …, and so on, as each key's hints show. The down flicks, and the free sides of ( 0 ., hold the ASCII symbols the English page has no keys for: @ & _ ~ ; ' " ` { } ! ?.
- In dictation mode the kana page gets Esc in the side column's top slot and Send in 😊's place.

Flick and toggle typing happen on release; other keys still act on press. A copy of the flick layout without the 「あA1」 key keeps the toolbar's あ / A button, which switches between kana and English.

## Prediction

While a reading of two kana or more is typed, the live candidates also complete it to common words, up to three, commonest first: おめでと offers おめでとう and おめでとうございます. They come first only while the reading is no word itself, unless it ends in った or んだ, a verb's past (あった before あったかい, やった before ヤッター, しんだ 死んだ before 診断), or a clearly rarer word of three kana or more, as with おめでと, or an adjective's stem, as おもしろ completes to 面白い, but not a noun one い longer (協会 doesn't lead 強化 for きょうか); a reading the list lacks that the converter reads whole as a word leads all but clearly commoner completions (年始 for ねんし, not 年式); a word and a particle counts as the word, a little less surely, so きょうは stays 今日は while ありが completes to ありがとう; and never over a conversion you committed for the reading before. The common chat phrases below complete a reading too, ranked as fairly common words, a word the list has too well ahead: いただき offers いただきます, ひさし 久しぶり and こんに こんにちは before 今日, while こんや stays 今夜, and a phrase only the list has doesn't lead a word and a particle (くるまで stays 車で before 車で来た), nor does any completion lead a stretched word or a katakana word Anthy reads whole (だめー is ダメー before ダメージ, こーら コーラ before コーラス); and otherwise after its conversion and your learned words, as ありがとう's completion ありがとうございます and きみ's 君たち do; a reading that is one word also keeps its likeliest other words before them, as あめ offers 雨 and 飴 before 編める. A completion never turns the reading's last kana into another sound with a small kana: きみ does not offer 奇妙 (きみょう). A tap commits the word in place of the reading. The 20,000 words come from Mozc's open-source dictionary (see `japanese/README.md` and the third-party notices); the list is read on first use and kept in memory, about 1 MB, only once Japanese is typed.

Common chat phrases convert as people usually write them, ahead of Anthy's own order: いきます to 行きます, かえる to 帰る, ありがとう stays in hiragana, ちょっとまって to ちょっと待って. Such a phrase is the first live candidate, and 変換 converts to it, as one segment where Anthy would split the reading otherwise; shortening that segment keeps the phrase's text and cuts kana off its end into a segment of their own, as written, with Anthy's conversions of them among its candidates (今日はいい天気だ | ね), and lengthening it joins them again. Once you commit something else for one of these readings more often, your choice wins and the usual phrase comes second, as 生きます then 行きます (kept in `japanese-choices.tsv` in the config folder, cleared by **Clear learned words**). Within a sentence, each of Anthy's segments takes a listed word's usual form too, before a particle as well (から, かも, けど, だ, ぞ, ぜ, じゃん and the like): すぐいく is すぐ行く where Anthy offers 育, いくね 行くね and いくよ 行くよ rather than 幾夜. Where Anthy reads the particle as part of a common word, that word stays: くるまで is 車で rather than 来るまで. A word the list keeps in hiragana, such as うん or いい, keeps it on its own and before a particle that ends a sentence (いいじゃん), but not before one a noun takes, where its kanji may be meant, nor before って, which also ends a verb's て form: うんがいい is 運がいい, and はいっていい 入っていい. After a case particle, such as が, the list can give a word another form, in entries marked 〜 that apply only so: 〜があう is listed as 〜が合う, so じかんがあう is 時間が合う, while あしたあう is 明日会う; 〜をかえる gives 色を変える, 〜をきてる 服を着てる, and 〜であう 場所で会う, also where Anthy puts the particle at the start of the next segment (場所|であう) or inside one (仕事であった, 仕事で会った), at the end of the phrase: 学校であった話 keeps Anthy's reading, and a particle isn't doubled (駅で出会った). On its own, であう stays 出会う. A listed word Anthy leaves in kana at a segment's end, after に, へ, と or から, takes it there as well (食べに行こう, 学校から帰る), but not where the whole segment is a word Anthy kept in kana (からかう); not after で, which できる begins with (ご飯できた), and after て it stays kana, as 〜ていく and 〜てくる are written (持っていく). The form you commit for such a word counts as your choice for it as well, also in a sentence the list has. Inside a sentence the word follows your latest choice, as on the iPhone, with the usual form next among its candidates: once you commit 往く for いく, かれもいく is 彼も往く, and one 行く brings 彼も行く back. Typed on its own, the form you chose most often comes first, the latest one on a tie. Picking a listed phrase from the live candidates counts as much as committing it through 変換. Where Anthy splits a sentence in the wrong place (友達|似合う), only the whole phrase in the list helps; such phrases (車で来た for くるまできた, which Anthy reads 車できた) are listed whole. A word stretched with ー, as chat writes it, converts as the word with the ー kept on its end: いいよー, だよねー, 分かるー; also a ー before a final い or う (ばいばーい, 知らなーい), or in a phrase the list has, before another final kana (行ってきまーす). If conversion or resizing splits the final kana into its own segment, the stretch marks stay at the end of the preceding segment, in its reading and every candidate: 凄ー | い. If the final kana still ends a longer segment, the stretch stays before it within that segment. An adjective stretched in place of its い comes as typed, its stem next: かわいー, then 可愛ー. Anthy's own katakana words, such as カレー and コーヒー, stay as they are, and a loanword of three kana or more, or with ー, comes as the word list writes it: こんびに is コンビニ, でーと デート. The list, `japanese/preferred.tsv`, holds about 460 phrases, short words, short replies such as はい and うん, and word forms, such as ここ, which stays in hiragana rather than 個々, and おれ, 俺.

After a reading's emoji come all its kaomoji, from Mozc's emoticon list: にこにこ offers ＼(^o^)／, (^o^) and 63 more, しくしく (T_T) and others; ∨ shows those past the row. Typing かお or かおもじ offers all 442. A kaomoji is picked like an emoji, in place of the reading, which ↶ brings back.

After a commit, while nothing is typed, the candidate row offers what may come next, as a phone's does: up to three phrases you committed straight after this one before, most often first, then 。 、 ！ ？. A tap types it at once; a phrase then offers what followed it in turn, and punctuation ends the run. Any other press, or the keyboard hiding, ends the offer, and the next commit makes it again.

## Learning

Japanese input always learns, as Steam's own keyboard does. With [Mozc](#mozc) converting, Mozc learns the picks in its own profile, as Anthy otherwise does in its record:

- A phrase committed from a conversion teaches Anthy which candidate each segment took, so they come first next time.
- Live candidates also offer words committed before that begin with the reading, right after the whole phrase: once ありがとう was committed as 有難う, typing あり offers 有難う. These are only words you committed; Anthy has no dictionary of its own for predictions.
- A phrase committed straight after another, with nothing else sent to the field between them, is offered after that one next time (see [Prediction](#prediction)). Up to 2,000 such pairs are kept, the oldest going first, in plain text in `japanese-next-words.tsv` in the config folder (`~/.config/framekeyboard`). Punctuation and dictation mode's editor take no part.
- Nothing is learned in a field that says it is for a password, a PIN or a terminal, as a [shell](integration.md)'s fields can. Steam's own fields don't say, so there, as on Steam's keyboard, phrases are learned.

Anthy keeps what it learns in plain text, in `~/.anthy/last-record1_framekeyboard.utf8` and `last-record2_framekeyboard.utf8`. Full Keyboard customises its candidates beyond Anthy's own order, so it keeps this record apart from `last-record1_default.utf8` and `last-record2_default.utf8`, which Steam's keyboard learns into, and doesn't disturb what other keyboards have learned. The first time it starts, it begins its record from a copy of Steam's keyboard's, read under Anthy's lock and left as it is, so the words you taught Steam's keyboard come along; after that the two learn apart. A marker, `japanese-learning-imported` in the config folder, keeps the copy from being made again, also after a clear. A phone IME's learned words sit in storage other apps can't open, but Steam Frame's desktop is an unlocked Linux system, where any program you run can read the files in your home folder, and a key the keyboard could use without asking you would be open to them as well, so the record isn't encrypted.

With Japanese as the main language or an additional one, Settings → Languages and Layouts → **Clear learned words** deletes Full Keyboard's record, the next phrases and the phrase choices, after asking. Steam's keyboard's record stays as it is, and nothing is copied from it again. It can't be pressed while there is nothing to clear. Anthy here restarts to forget what it had loaded; meanwhile a ring circles the button, which can't be pressed again, and live candidates wait.

## Mozc

Kanji conversion uses Mozc, the open-source engine of Google Japanese Input, where the release has it, and Anthy otherwise. Mozc weighs the whole sentence, so it splits and converts sentences far better than Anthy does: on a blind set of 2,562 everyday chat phrases, the layer described above gave the expected first candidate for 83.5% of them on Mozc and for 69.4% on Anthy, and for 76% of long sentences against 52%; with the rules below, 85.9% on Mozc, and 82.5% on a second blind set of 10,308. The phrase list's usual forms, stretched words, completions and the other live candidates all work on Mozc's segments as on Anthy's; where Mozc reads a particle as part of a word, that word stays (京都旅行, not 今日と旅行). Mozc writes digits, Latin letters and % half-width: 2日, Tシャツ, 10%. Words its dictionary lacks or reads formally, in `japanese/mozc-words.tsv` (陽キャ, リアタイ, クレカ, 推し, VR, DM), are given to it as a user dictionary, `user_dictionary.db` in its profile, written as it starts, so it segments sentences around them: ようきゃのあつまり is 陽キャの集まり, ぶいあーるごーぐる VRゴーグル. Kana words it would write in kanji (うち) are there too. It reads numbers spelled in kana as words (三重五分 for さんじゅうごふん), so a number spelled before a counter goes to it as digits, which it converts as chat writes them: さんじゅっぷん is 30分, いちまんえん 1万円, じゅうがつはつか 10月20日. A digit alone counts only where it and its counter make no common word (いちばん, いっぱい and にほん stay, but not before another number: ごじごじゅっぷん is 5時50分), and mid-sentence only after a particle, て, or a word such as もう, あと, ちょうど or やく (もう4年, 約260年); に, よ, く and し count mostly at the reading's start, as に is mostly a particle, and after a time of day or a weekday the hour does (夕方5時, 毎晩10時, 夜中の2時, 火曜日の4時), as does くじ before から, まで or ごろ (9時から; くじに当たった stays). Where a counter has a number's shorter start before it, that is the number: さんじゅうにん is 30人, not 32 and ん. A number of more than one part counts without a counter too (400, 260から), but not a digit and せん alone, which may be 戦 (6戦), nor one ending in に, the particle perhaps (二重に). Decimals before a counter (いってんごばい, 1.5倍, れいてんさんびょう 0.3秒), phone numbers spelled digit by digit (ぜろきゅうぜろ, 090) and days without a month (じゅうよっか, 14日; not 二日酔い) come as digits too, as does any day after a month with の, after another day with から or まで, or before の and a time of day (5月の3日から5日まで, 5日の午後3時; いつか alone stays someday). A unit alone takes 点 too (100点, 10点), and a digit alone counts after 震度. Where Mozc itself reads kana as a number in kanji numerals and offers it in digits, the digits come first: 四年も as 4年も, 二百六十|年 as 260|年, 三件 3件, 第二回 第2回, 百円 100円, 一日|三回 1日3回; but not words such as 一度, 一体 or 一番, and not 五 in 五重塔. A score's second number (3対1), 分 after a temperature (38度5分) and 2万 where Mozc offers only 二万 come in digits too, and 回 is 階 for a floor: after 地下, or before a room, a lift and the like (6階のエレベーター, 5階の502号室). Where Mozc puts the noun and the verb apart and reads the verb wrongly for its object, the right one among its candidates comes first: 雪|降ってきた, 写真を|撮って, 傘|貸して, 服を|着てみる, ギター|弾ける, 猫|飼ってる, 目が|覚めた, 電話|鳴ってた, 雨が|やんだ; and so does the noun its verb asks: かぜ before ひく is 風邪, せきが before でる 咳が, ぼうしを before かぶる 帽子を. The object may stand a few segments back, across words that are no other object: この写真は|ちょうど|10年前に|撮った, but 写真と傘を|取った. More pairs decide each other the same way: 試合に勝つ, 家に着く, 貸したら返ってこない, 足が速い, お金が貯まる, 風邪が流行る, 定時で上がる, 公園を走る, 幹事を頼む, 地震は震度, 一杯飲む. Words chat writes in kana where Mozc writes kanji come in kana where Mozc offers them so: ください, できる, よかった, もらう, いる, よく, なくす, ちょうだい and つらい (read so, not からい), while 上出来 and 仲良く stay; and words chat writes in kanji where Mozc writes kana come in kanji: 時 after a clause (暇な時; とき alone stays), の方が and た方が (ないほうが stays), ご飯, 疲れ, 忙しい, 滑る, 曇り, and 置いた (〜ておいた stays). うち read so stays うち rather than 家, ネタ ending a clause is 寝た (やっと寝たから), おき after a time is in kana (15分おき), a word spelled out stays so (ゴールデンウィーク, not GW), and から before からかさ, which Mozc shows as 傘 alone, is kept (雨らしいから傘).

Mozc runs as a program of its own, `lib/framekeyboard/framekeyboard-mozc`, only while Japanese is the main language or an additional one: it starts when Japanese is added in Settings and stops when it is removed, so it uses no memory otherwise, and about 20–30 MB while it runs. It keeps its profile in `mozc` in the config folder (`~/.config/framekeyboard/mozc`), apart from any Mozc on the system, and talks to the keyboard over a local socket; nothing leaves the device. Where bubblewrap is installed, as on SteamOS, it runs in a sandbox that sees the system read-only and can write only its profile. There it also sits at the path Mozc's own watchdog checks, `/usr/lib/mozc/mozc_server`; started anywhere else, Mozc aborts itself every three minutes. Without bubblewrap, or where bubblewrap itself fails, it runs as it is and is started again each time it aborts. A conversion waits for it at most 0.3 s, Anthy converting otherwise. If it crashes, or doesn't answer in time twice, a second apart, with no answer between, Anthy converts meanwhile and Mozc is started again within seconds. One that keeps stopping within a minute of its start is started again later each time, and after five starts not again until Japanese is added again or the keyboard restarts; if a release lacks it, Anthy converts for good. Mozc learns the candidates picked in each phrase committed, as Anthy does, in its profile; **Clear learned words** clears that too ([Learning](#learning)). What Anthy learned stays Anthy's: it isn't carried over to Mozc.

## Runtime requirements

The integrated modes use the release's Mozc server ([Mozc](#mozc)), `libanthy.so.0` with its installed dictionary, and Japanese fonts. Anthy and the fonts were already present on the tested Steam Frame. No daemon besides that server, no browser, online converter, clipboard fallback or dictionary training is added. With [learning](#learning) turned off, Anthy learns nothing from your choices, so nothing you type is written to disk. Live candidates convert the reading once after each pause in typing, never while you type; ordinary polling/rendering stays adaptive. The library/dictionary load with the first conversion, live or requested, and stay loaded until the keyboard exits.

The normal launcher with `--input ei --start-enabled` supports the bundled integrated profiles. Japanese characters are composed locally. The normal launcher does not require a matching system keymap; native shortcuts use the compositor keymap where available. The text backend checks the private [Gamescope input-method protocol](https://github.com/ValveSoftware/gamescope/blob/6867f509874f9bc52e12d6f4c4596cdf0d5be6b4/protocol/gamescope-input-method.xml) before enabling output. Missing support leaves a conversion preview and a visible error. The uinput backend cannot deliver composed Japanese text. A numbered `--ei-socket` selects the matching text compositor; custom names need `--text-socket`. See [runtime routing](runtime.md).

External JIS is for a session already using the Japanese physical keymap and a working system IME. Close the running keyboard before changing launch options:

```sh
~/.local/bin/framekeyboard --vr --input ei --target-language ja-jis --start-enabled
```

Then select JIS (system IME) in Settings. Selecting JIS while still declaring a US target disables output; it never changes the system layout/IME silently. The geometry uses a tall rectangular Enter key.

## Current limits and validation

Reading is limited to roughly 30 Unicode characters per phrase, with at most 32 in one native commit. Candidate lists expose up to 128 alternatives per segment, plus the two scripts. Drag the candidate row sideways to see more; a quick drag glides on, like the emoji picker, and the thumbstick scrolls it too. Full paragraph editing, predictions from earlier choices (they need Anthy to learn, which Full Keyboard does not do), custom learned dictionaries, half-width kana conversion and repeat while holding composition keys other than Backspace are not implemented. A held Backspace repeats on the reading, in the keyboard and in the dictation editor, and stops once the reading is empty. The desktop preview composes locally and never sends OS input.

Host and ARM64 tests cover romaji, voiced/small kana, candidate selection, scripts, failed commit retention, cancellation, opt-in and modifier routing. The dedicated on-device Xwayland receiver verified 日本 via the native UTF-8 connection. That does not establish every nested desktop/browser or external system-IME combination; test a disposable field in the desired application.
